The deadline is 31 December 2026. Every state-funded mainstream school in England receiving the Inclusive Mainstream Fund (IMF) must have a published inclusion strategy live on its website by then. That is a condition of the grant — not an aspiration.

With 51 days to go, many schools are still drafting. This guide sets out exactly what the strategy must contain, how the DfE's seven principles apply, and what a strategy that holds up at Ofsted inspection actually looks like.

---

## Why this matters now

The requirement comes from the Schools White Paper *Every Child Achieving and Thriving*, published in February 2026. It is the centrepiece of the government's shift towards "inclusive by design" mainstream schools — moving away from reactive SEND support and towards a whole-school, cohort-level approach.

The IMF is new money: £400 million a year for three years, shared across all eligible mainstream schools and 16-to-19 academies in England. Funding rates for 2026/27 are:

- £3,000 per school (base allocation)
- £16 per primary pupil
- £79 for each pupil with low prior attainment
- an area cost adjustment on top

A typical mid-sized primary — say, 250 pupils with a reasonable proportion of low prior attainment — could receive £14,000 or more. School business managers can check exact allocations in [the DfE's published IMF allocations](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-mainstream-fund-2026-to-2027).

Crucially, unused 2026/27 allocation can be carried forward provided it is spent by 31 March 2028 and continues to meet the conditions of grant — so there is no pressure to rush spending, but the *strategy* must still be published by 31 December.

## What the strategy must include

The DfE provides a [template](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-mainstream-fund-support-for-school-leaders/developing-an-inclusion-strategy-using-the-inclusive-mainstream-fund), but you can use your own format provided it covers four required areas:

1. **The commonly occurring barriers to learning and participation across your cohort.** This is not a list of individual pupils or diagnoses. The DfE wants a cohort-level analysis — what barriers appear repeatedly and predictably in your school? Evidence should draw on assessment data, attendance patterns, behaviour logs, classroom observations, pupil voice, and family feedback.

2. **The activities you are investing in to alleviate those barriers, with budgeted costs.** Every activity funded in full or in part from IMF must map to at least one of the seven principles (see below). The DfE's guidance is explicit: costs should be stated.

3. **Intended outcomes.** What will improve, for whom, and over what timescale? The pupil premium strategy statement is the closest analogue — the same logic of identify, invest, measure, review applies here.

4. **A review of the previous academic year.** For the first cycle (2026/27), there is no prior-year IMF to review, so schools can note this and focus instead on existing inclusion data as the baseline.

If you write a multi-year strategy, it must be reviewed annually, with the review noted on the cover page.

## The seven principles of inclusion

Every pound of IMF spending must align with at least one of the DfE's seven principles. Schools do not need to spend across all seven — the guidance recommends starting with a small number of high-impact activities that address your identified barriers.

| Principle | What it covers |
|---|---|
| 1. Ambitious leadership and governance | Trustees, governors and senior leaders creating accountability structures for inclusion |
| 2. Evidence-based support and early intervention | Using data to identify need early; interventions grounded in evidence |
| 3. High-quality adaptive teaching | Curriculum designed for all learners; scaffolding; accessible pedagogy |
| 4. Accessible and enriching provision beyond the classroom | Extracurricular, trips, enrichment activities accessible to all pupils |
| 5. Safe, respectful culture, belonging and attendance | Reducing exclusions; attendance support; anti-bullying; pupil wellbeing |
| 6. Strong family and wider-service partnerships | Working with parents/carers, health, social care, and community organisations |
| 7. Inclusive environments and accessibility | Physical accessibility; assistive technology; sensory-friendly adaptations |

A school that has identified, for example, that a significant number of pupils struggle to engage during independent work might map spending on adaptive teaching CPD to Principle 3. A school where attendance is the clearest barrier maps its targeted family support to Principles 5 and 6.

## The connection to MAT governance

For multi-academy trusts, the picture has an extra layer. The Academy Trust Handbook 2026 (effective 1 October 2026) adds new statutory expectations: trusts must designate a trustee or committee with specific oversight responsibility for SEND, and inclusion must be embedded across all aspects of provision. Each individual school in a trust must still publish its own strategy — cluster pooling of IMF is permitted where governance allows, but it does not remove the per-school publishing requirement.

This means trust SEND leads and designated SEND trustees need line of sight into each school's strategy, and need to be confident the evidence base is solid before December.

## What Ofsted will look for

Inclusion now has its own explicit focus in the Ofsted inspection framework that came into effect from September 2026. Inspectors will look at:

- how well the school identifies barriers to learning across its whole cohort, not just those with EHCPs
- the quality of adaptive teaching and whether the curriculum is genuinely designed for all learners
- how the school works in partnership with families and external services
- the evidence trail — does the school know what it invested, in what, and what changed?

A published inclusion strategy that is thin on evidence or disconnected from the school's actual data will not satisfy this. The strategy is the starting point, not the endpoint: Ofsted will want to see that it reflects what is genuinely happening in classrooms.

## Five practical steps before 31 December

The DfE's own guidance recommends a five-step process: **identify → examine the evidence → develop your strategy → implement and monitor → evaluate**. With seven weeks to the deadline, here is a realistic sequencing:

1. **Pull your cohort data now.** Assessment, attendance, exclusion/suspension rates, pupil voice surveys, teacher and TA observations. What patterns emerge that do not depend on individual EHCP status?
2. **Map to the seven principles.** Of your identified barriers, which principles are most relevant? This shapes where your IMF spending goes.
3. **Cost your activities.** Be specific — CPD sessions, resources, additional staffing hours, environmental adaptations. The DfE expects budgeted costs in the published strategy.
4. **Write the strategy in plain language.** It is a public document. Parents and carers should be able to read it and understand what the school is doing and why.
5. **Publish before 31 December.** The DfE's template is available; nurtureuk also offers a [free template](https://www.nurtureuk.org/inclusion-strategy-for-schools/) built around the required sections and seven principles.

---

## A word of caution

The DfE is explicit that the IMF should not simply subsidise provision schools were already paying for. If your school already subscribes to a resource or intervention, IMF cannot just replace that budget line. Any activity funded must be a genuine addition or expansion that addresses an identified barrier — and the strategy must make that link visible.

The direction of travel is also firmly towards more, not less, accountability. The DfE has signalled that a statutory duty to produce an inclusion strategy — described as more comprehensive than the current condition-of-grant requirement — is planned for the future. What schools build now is the foundation they will be measured against.

From 1 January 2027, every central government contract worth £1 million or more — departments, executive agencies and non-departmental public bodies — must comply with [Procurement Policy Note 026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a727128332792bd88ece51b/PPN_026_The_Social_Value_Model.pdf), the government's new Social Value Model. The Cabinet Office published implementation guidance on 28–29 October 2026 to help commissioners put it into practice. For anyone who commissions, bids for or evaluates public contracts, the measurement and evidence obligations have changed significantly.

## What PPN 026 replaces and what has changed

PPN 026 replaces PPN 002, which had governed social value assessment since 2020. The core changes affect weightings, structure and accountability.

Under PPN 002, a flat 10% minimum weighting applied to all in-scope contracts. PPN 026 introduces a banded approach:

| Contract value (inc. VAT) | Minimum social value weighting | Social value KPI |
|---|---|---|
| £1m to under £5m | 10% | Encouraged, not required |
| £5m and above | **20%** | At least one, published |

The doubling of the minimum weighting on larger contracts is significant. On a £10m contract scored out of 100, social value is now worth at least 20 marks — the same as many technical quality criteria. Organisations that treat social value as a box-ticking exercise are exposed to real competitive disadvantage.

Structurally, the new model is built around **two Outcomes** and **six Model Award Criteria (MACs)**:

- **Outcome 1 — Good jobs:** Create high-quality jobs (1a); fair working conditions (1b); fair pay (1c)
- **Outcome 2 — Skills and opportunities:** Create opportunities for people facing barriers to employment (2a); develop skills (2b); strengthen communities (2c)

Each MAC has a set of **Standard Reporting Metrics (SRMs)** — 37 in total. These are the standard units commissioners use to set and track KPIs during delivery.

## How social value will be evaluated at tender stage

This is where many commissioners and suppliers get caught out. Under PPN 026, evaluation is **qualitative by default**, not quantitative. Buyers are instructed to score how convincing a method statement and implementation plan are — not simply how large the promised numbers are.

Quantities still matter: they go into the contract and are monitored against KPIs. On contracts of £5m or more, buyers may also score quantities under MAC 1a (jobs). But in most cases, a credible, well-evidenced plan beats an implausible large headline figure. The [October 2026 guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ae1ea86bdb574cf1c9b3f10/2026-10-28_PPN_026_The_Social_Value_Model_guidance.pdf) sets out an example scoring methodology with five confidence levels, from "no confidence" to "excellent confidence — every criterion met, with additional, relevant social value offered."

For commissioners designing tender evaluation frameworks, this means:

- **Write clear scoring criteria** that reward specificity, local knowledge and delivery realism, not volume of promises
- **Require method statements** that name the target cohorts, local community programmes and delivery mechanisms
- **Build in a due diligence step** for unusually high commitments — the guidance warns against accepting implausible quantities

## The KPI and monitoring obligations from award

For contracts of £5m or more, at least one social value KPI must be set at award and **published on the Central Digital Platform** (Find a Tender). This is a material change. Social value performance is now publicly visible, creating accountability that did not exist under PPN 002.

The KPIs must use SRMs where one exists for the relevant MAC. That means commissioners and their suppliers need to agree, before contract signature, how data will be collected, by whom, with what frequency, and against what baseline. Too many contracts reach the KPI-setting stage without having resolved these questions, leading to either weak KPIs or undeliverable ones.

Good practice at this stage includes:

- **Setting a baseline** using local or national labour market data before the contract starts
- **Agreeing data collection responsibilities** contractually — who reports, in what format, by when
- **Applying standard adjustments** where relevant: deadweight (what would have happened anyway), attribution (what other factors contributed) and displacement (whether benefits to one group come at the cost of another)
- **Publishing performance updates** at regular intervals, not just at contract end

This matters for evaluation, not just contract management. If KPI data is collected inconsistently, it cannot support any credible assessment of whether social value was actually delivered.

## Why evidence quality is now a live risk

The October 2026 guidance introduces a feedback loop: published KPI performance on completed contracts becomes visible to future buyers assessing new bids. Suppliers with a track record of delivering — with evidence — will be better placed than those making large promises they cannot substantiate.

For commissioners, this creates a new due diligence obligation: **checking the evidence base behind social value claims**, not just the claims themselves. The guidance notes that financial proxies (such as those used in Social Return on Investment calculations) can be useful context, but they are not a substitute for empirical data showing that outcomes were actually achieved.

The five social value measurement standards that remain most relevant under PPN 026 are:

1. **Specificity** — outcomes stated at a level of detail that could be independently verified
2. **Deadweight** — credible estimate of what would have happened without the intervention
3. **Attribution** — honest account of which outcomes are genuinely caused by this contract
4. **Duration** — how long benefits are expected to last, with evidence for the assumption
5. **Additionality** — net benefit after accounting for displacement and substitution effects

These are not academic concepts. They are the questions any robust evaluation of a PPN 026 contract will need to answer.

## Practical steps for commissioners now

PPN 026 applies to procurements **commenced** on or after 1 January 2027. That means tender notices issued from that date. If you are planning a contract to be let in spring 2027, the procurement planning stage — including social value design — needs to happen now.

Key actions before the January cut-off:

- **Review contracts in pipeline** to confirm which fall within scope (central government bodies, £1m+, commenced under the Procurement Act 2023)
- **Design social value criteria** around the two Outcomes and six MACs — the guidance includes a worked scoring example
- **Plan KPI infrastructure** for any contract likely to reach £5m — agree data collection protocols before award
- **Build evaluation into contract design**, not as an afterthought — especially for multi-year contracts where learning should inform future iterations

For local authorities, housing associations and other public bodies not directly subject to PPN 026, this model is worth watching. Many already apply the Social Value Act 2012 and local equivalents; the PPN 026 framework offers a more structured approach to measurement that can be adapted outside the central government context.

---

*PPN 026 applies to central government procurements commenced from 1 January 2027. The Cabinet Office guidance published 28–29 October 2026 covers procurement planning, tender evaluation, scoring methodology and contract management. Local government bodies are not currently required to follow PPN 026 but remain subject to the Public Services (Social Value) Act 2012.*
